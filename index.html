Kingdom Manga: Story, Historical Setting, Anime and Ratings

Kingdom is a historical action manga written and illustrated by Yasuhisa Hara. Set during China’s Warring States period, it follows Xin (Shin), a war orphan who dreams of becoming a great general, and Ying Zheng, the young ruler of Qin who aims to unite the warring states. The series combines battlefield strategy, political rivalries, military ambition, and personal growth.

Story and main characters

Xin begins as a poor village boy with an extraordinary ambition. After the death of his childhood friend Piao and a chance encounter with the young king Ying Zheng, Xin becomes involved in the struggle to restore Qin’s royal authority. As he rises through the military ranks, he faces rival generals, shifting alliances, difficult campaigns, and the human cost of war. Ying Zheng’s political goal and Xin’s military dream develop alongside each other, giving the story both court intrigue and large-scale battlefield action.

Historical inspiration and genre

The manga draws inspiration from the late Warring States era and the historical process that led to Qin’s unification of China. It uses historical figures and events as a foundation while dramatizing dialogue, battles, and character relationships. Its main genres include historical action, military drama, strategy, politics, and coming-of-age storytelling.

Does Kingdom have an anime?

Yes. Kingdom has a television anime adaptation that began in 2012, with later seasons continuing the story. The earliest season is often discussed for its heavy use of early CGI, while later seasons changed production approach and expanded the adaptation. The anime is a separate viewing experience from the manga, and the manga provides the original artwork and detailed visual storytelling.

Kingdom ratings

Ratings vary by platform and by season. AniList’s manga listing has shown a score around 89/100, while a third-party anime database has listed the first TV season around 7.4/10; these figures are snapshots rather than permanent scores. Later seasons may receive different ratings, so check the exact season and current community page before comparing.

Why Kingdom stands out

Kingdom is especially known for its strategic battles, ambitious generals, large armies, shifting alliances, and the gradual development of Xin from an inexperienced fighter into a serious military leader. Readers who enjoy military strategy, political maneuvering, and long character arcs may find the series particularly engaging. Because it is a long-running manga, beginning at Chapter 1 gives the clearest understanding of the characters and their motivations.
